# LOGIC: how the skill tree's prerequisites work

## Rules
- **Hard requirement (`all`)**: every one must be cleared. These are drawn as **thorny green vines**.
- **Any-of group (`any`)**: clearing **one** item in the group is enough. These are drawn as **smooth yellow vines**. A chapter can have several groups; each needs one.
- **Fork (`excl`)**: you pick one option (runway source: consulting or day job; capital: bootstrap or venture). "Switch to this path" undoes the other option and refunds it.
- **"I've already done this"**: available on any chapter, even a locked one. It gives credit and gold so experienced founders can skip ahead honestly. It respects forks.

## Design reasoning
Hard requirements are used only where skipping a step would genuinely mislead you:
1. **First Swing → Problem Hunting → Mom Test interviews → 10 Real Conversations.** Everything about the product depends on having really talked to customers. Positioning and MVP scope hard-require this chain.
2. **Fork-specific follow-ups** (e.g. Default Alive Math needs the Bootstrap path; Pitch Deck needs the Venture path).
3. **Paid ads need a first customer plus analytics.** Paying for traffic without a converting funnel and tracking is the classic money pit.
4. **PMF milestone needs Retention**, and Retention needs a first customer. You can't measure "very disappointed" without active users.

Everything else uses any-of groups, so founders can take different routes:
- **Landing Page** opens from Positioning **or** a Smoke Test **or** the 10-conversations milestone.
- **Payments** opens from an MVP **or** a landing page (pre-orders count).
- **Legal basics** open from a landing page, payments, the MVP scope, **or** consulting (consultants need an entity first).
- **Personal brand** opens right after Problem Hunting. Building an audience early is the best launch prep, so it is no longer gated behind a first customer.
- **First Paying Customer** comes through **any** launch move: private beta, Product Hunt, Show HN, Indie Hackers, cold outbound, or your supporters list.

### The launch gate (`gate-launch`, "Ready to Launch")
Launching requires only what's truly necessary. Four essentials:
1. **Talked to users**: 10 Real Conversations (hard)
2. **Legal basics**: Legal & Admin (hard)
3. **Something to show**: Landing Page **or** Build the MVP
4. **A way to capture value**: Payments **or** Smoke Test/Waitlist (a free or waitlist launch is valid)

Analytics, onboarding polish, pricing tiers, support docs and weekly shipping are **not** required. They earn gold and bonuses but never block launch. The LAUNCH meter in the top bar shows these four essentials (0/4 → 4/4). All public launch chapters (beta, Product Hunt, Show HN, Indie Hackers) require the gate. The Launch Prep Checklist and Rally 30 Supporters do not, because you prepare those *before* the gate.

## Validator (`node tools/validate-graph.js`)
Checks:
- unknown or duplicate ids, self-references and missing fields
- empty or single-option any-groups (single options belong in `all`)
- **cycles** (DFS)
- **reachability under every fork combination** (4 combos); fails if a chapter can never be reached
- redundant hard requirements (already implied by another hard requirement)
- any-group options already guaranteed by a hard requirement, and dominated options (one option implies another in the same group)
- **silly gates**: a hard requirement on a fork option from outside that fork's own branch, which would lock out players who chose the other side
- the fewest chapters needed to clear every milestone

Current output, summarized:
- Fewest chapters to clear each milestone: 10 Conversations = 4, Ready to Launch = 9, First Paying Customer = 11, PMF = 13, Ramen Profitable = 14, Term Sheet = 16.
- Fork combos: Bootstrap players reach every chapter except the five Venture chapters, and vice versa (by design). Day-job + Venture players can't reach Productized Services, which is a bootstrap/consulting play.
